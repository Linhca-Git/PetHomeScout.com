# PetHomeScout US Audience & UI Specification

Source: supplied project master knowledge base, Project Version 2026.2.

## Audience

PetHomeScout serves US households in US English, USD, square feet, inches, pounds, ZIP codes, cities, and states. The primary buyer is a woman aged 35–65 who treats pets as family and wants fast, trustworthy evidence before buying higher-ticket home and pet technology.

The product is an independent decision engine, not a generic pet blog. The emotional promise is reassurance: help people make a confident choice for a cleaner, safer home without hype or hidden commercial pressure.

## UI direction

- Use a hybrid editorial/data system: warm serif display headings paired with Inter-like sans-serif UI, specs, tables, and labels.
- Favor a bright white canvas, pale blue-gray section surfaces, deep navy utility/header bars, cobalt primary actions, green score/success states, and amber disclosure/warning states.
- Use clear hierarchy, generous whitespace, short scan-friendly summaries, score badges, ranked cards, and lightweight list rows.
- Use recognizable line-style icons for pets, home, cleaning, safety, insurance, and tools. Avoid childish paw-print decoration, abstract geometric glyphs, coupon styling, and crowded affiliate-blog layouts.
- Make the primary action visible in the hero. Support quick scanning with score badges, category cards, comparison rows, and a concise disclosure near commercial actions.
- Design responsive breakpoints around desktop 1280px, tablet 768–1279px, and mobile below 768px. Avoid horizontal overflow; comparison tables may scroll inside a bounded container.

## Initial homepage composition

1. Navy utility bar with US edition/context.
2. White navigation header with Reviews, Buying Guides, Comparisons, Categories, Product Database, Deals, Blog, and newsletter CTA.
3. Two-column hero: authority headline, two CTAs, trust signals, and a featured product/guide card with image, score, and editorial pick label.
4. Explore Categories rail.
5. Four-card Editor's Picks grid with rank and score badges.
6. Three lightweight list columns: Latest Reviews, Buying Guides, Popular Comparisons.
7. Trust/editorial methodology section.
8. Navy newsletter CTA.
9. Footer with disclosure, editorial policy, privacy, contact, and CCPA/Do Not Sell or Share links.

## Conversion and trust rules

- Never put direct affiliate URLs in content. Future commercial links use internal `/go/` redirects.
- Show affiliate disclosure near the first commercial action and below the article H1 where relevant.
- Lead forms must use multi-step progression and a visible TCPA consent statement at the final submit action.
- Never invent US offices, testing claims, ratings, reviews, authors, or coverage guarantees.
- Keep the operator identity transparent as a global digital business; use official domain email addresses once they exist.

## SEO implications

- Build topical clusters around family-home safety, smart pet tech, cleaning/odor, and services/insurance.
- Use root-level URLs and US-specific search intent, including breed-specific and household-problem queries.
- Prefer people-first evergreen pages and useful comparison tools over mass-produced programmatic pages.
- Keep visible page claims aligned with schema, metadata, and the actual evidence shown.
